Focus on one series:

Rolling 120-month annualized return

Annualized mean log return over the trailing 120 months — (12/w) times the sum of the last w monthly log returns — recalculated each month, over the selected date range. In single-series mode, the shaded band is a ±1.96 SE (95%) confidence interval for that window length, and the dashed line is the full-sample estimate over the selected date range.

Rolling 120-month annualized volatility

Annualized standard deviation of monthly returns over the trailing 120 months, recalculated each month. Try shortening the window above: returns swing wildly while volatility stays comparatively stable — a reminder that risk is far more predictable than return.

Rolling 120-month skewness

Rolling 120-month excess kurtosis

Sample skewness and excess kurtosis over the trailing window, using the same definitions as Excel’s SKEW() and KURT() so the numbers can be reproduced in a spreadsheet. The grey band is the ±1.96 SE range a sample of T normal draws would produce, SE(skew) ≈ √(6/T) and SE(excess kurtosis) ≈ √(24/T); it assumes normality, and under fat tails the true SE of kurtosis is much larger. Red shading marks 1929–32, Oct 1987, 2000–02, Sep 2008–Mar 2009 and Mar 2020. Single-series mode only.

Both estimates use the same data. The mean's uncertainty depends on how many years you have; the vol's depends on how many observations.

Pairwise correlation of monthly returns

Computed over each pair's overlapping monthly history within the selected date range. Values near +1 move together; values near 0 or negative are diversifying. Use the legend above to change which series are included.

Rolling volatility of the first series

Correlation between the two series over a trailing window (36 months monthly, 52 weeks weekly, 252 days daily), with the first series’ rolling annualized volatility underneath. The dashed line is the full-sample correlation over the selected date range. Correlations are not constant, and they tend to rise when volatility spikes (red shading marks crises): diversification is weakest exactly when it is needed. The static table above uses the whole date range at once.

Distribution of standardized monthly returns (z-scores)

Each checked series is standardized by its own mean and standard deviation over the selected date range, so a z-score of 3 means “three of that series’ own standard deviations from its mean”. The dashed curve is a single N(0,1) normal. Because every series is on the same scale, departures from the curve measure fat tails and skew, not differences in volatility. The x-axis is fixed at ±6; z-scores beyond it pile into the end bins.

Tail counts

For each k, the number of months with |z| > k, next to the count a normal distribution predicts, 2TΦ(−k), and how often the normal model says such a month should occur. A series with fat tails shows many more extreme months than the normal expects, and the gap widens as k grows. Recomputes with the date range and the legend selection.

Autocorrelation of returns, r

Autocorrelation of absolute returns, |r|

Bars are the sample autocorrelation at each lag; the shaded band is ±1.96/√T, where about 95% of bars would fall if the series were unpredictable noise. Both charts share one scale. Returns sit inside the band: the direction of next period’s return cannot be predicted from this period’s. Absolute returns sit far above it and decay slowly: big moves follow big moves and quiet follows quiet, which is volatility clustering. Computed on log returns by default (see the log/simple toggle) over the selected date range.
